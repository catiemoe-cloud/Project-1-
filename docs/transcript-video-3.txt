In this video, I’ll be showing my final Apple recommendation and demonstrating how the model I created to evaluate Apple supports my recommendation. My recommendation is to watch and defer Apple. I would not initiate at the recorded market price of $337 per share as of September 17, 2026.

Looking at the base case, I used 6% revenue growth, a 47% gross margin, operating expense of 15%, capex of 3%, a WACC of 9%, and a 2.5% terminal growth. Using these assumptions, the model gives us a base value of $131.61 per share. The table below shows the five-year forecast for revenue, EBIT, FCFF, and the balance-sheet and cash-flow checks.

Next, I’m going to show how changing the revenue growth from 6% to 9% affects my model. If I change my revenue growth and run the new valuation, we can see that the value per share increases to $148.85.

This was my locked changed-input validation. Keeping everything else the same, higher revenue growth increases projected revenue, EBIT, and FCFF, which increases enterprise value. Even after this increase, I would still watch and defer Apple because $148.85 is still below the $337 market price.

Now I’ll show how easily accessible my model is. I can reset this base case. The original $131.61 value returns, showing that testing a different assumption does not silently change my original base case.

Still in the workbench, I can also show how I included an invalid-input check. Clicking on this button, you can see that a 5% WACC is outside the model’s allowable range of 6% minimum. So the workbench gives an input-check-failed message instead of creating a valuation. This is a guardrail that prevents unsupported inputs from producing a result.

The last thing I’d like to show the committee is my decision memo. The five-year FCFF model produces enterprise value of about $1.8 trillion. After adding cash and marketable securities, subtracting debt, and dividing by shares, the base equity value is $131.61 per share. Therefore, this base case is below by $25.39, leading to my watch-and-defer conclusion.

The low, base, and high values are $87, $131.61, and $185.85 per share. All three are below the recorded $337 market price as well. My peer cross-check also stayed below the market price, but I do not mechanically average these methods because the peer companies are not identical to Apple.

Now, at the bottom of the decision memo, my recommendation could change if future filings show stronger and sustainable Services growth, iPhone demand, Greater China performance, gross margin, or cash-flow conversion. I would also monitor capital spending and regulation.

If that evidence supports a stronger FCFF path, I would rebuild the forecast and reconsider initiating a position. The decision today is not that Apple is a bad company. It’s that under the documented assumptions and the current $337 market price, the risk-adjusted evidence does not support starting a position today. My committee recommendation is to defer initiation and revisit the company when either the operating evidence or the price-to-value comparison changes.
